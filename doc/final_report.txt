TMDb MOVIE DATA ANALYSIS -- FINAL REPORT
==========================================

1. OVERVIEW
------------
Fetches, cleans, and analyzes 19 movie IDs from the project brief via the
TMDb API, answers the required KPI questions, and produces supporting
visualizations. ID 0 is invalid and is skipped automatically; all results
below are based on the remaining N = 18 movies.

2. METHODOLOGY
----------------
- Fetch: one call per movie via append_to_response=credits (19 calls, not
  ~37) with retry/backoff on transient failures. Results are cached
  locally and re-fetched incrementally -- only IDs missing from the cache
  are downloaded on subsequent runs.
- Clean: dropped irrelevant columns; flattened nested JSON fields
  (genres, companies, countries, languages, collection) into '|'-joined
  strings; coerced types; treated budget/revenue/runtime of 0 and
  vote_average with 0 votes as missing, not real values.
- KPIs: budget/revenue converted to million USD; profit and ROI derived;
  every ranking (revenue, budget, profit, ROI, votes, rating, popularity)
  goes through one shared ranking function. ROI and "rated" rankings
  apply the brief's filters (budget >= $10M; vote_count >= 10) -- skipping
  these silently changes the answer.
- Visualize: 5 charts (Revenue vs. Budget, ROI by Genre, Popularity vs.
  Rating, Yearly Revenue Trend, Franchise vs. Standalone), saved to
  reports/figures/.
- Code: logic lives in an installed package (src/tmdb_pipeline/), not
  inline in scripts; 74 automated tests cover cleaning, KPIs, and fetch
  without hitting the real API. Full run: python scripts/run_pipeline.py.

3. KEY INSIGHTS
------------------
- Highest revenue: Avatar ($2,923.7M), Avengers: Endgame ($2,799.4M),
  Titanic ($2,264.2M) -- the same three lead profit too.
- Highest ROI (budget >= $10M): Avatar (12.3x), Titanic (11.3x),
  Jurassic World (11.1x). Lowest: Star Wars: The Last Jedi (4.4x).
- Highest rated (>= 10 votes): Avengers: Endgame and Infinity War (8.24),
  Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows: Part 2 (8.08).
- Rating and popularity correlate moderately (r = 0.79).
- Advanced searches (Bruce Willis sci-fi/action; Uma Thurman x Tarantino)
  return 0 matches -- expected, since this dataset is only the 18
  blockbuster franchise entries named in the brief, not the full catalog.
- Franchises dominate the sample (16 of 18 movies), with higher mean
  budget ($218M vs. $180M) and popularity, but standalone films edge out
  a better median ROI (9.6x vs. 7.8x) -- on just 2 movies, illustrative
  only, not a general claim about standalone films.
- Most successful franchise: The Avengers Collection ($7,776.1M total
  revenue across 4 movies). Most successful director: James Cameron
  (Avatar + Titanic, $5,187.9M); the Russo brothers (Endgame + Infinity
  War) are second and hold the highest average rating of any director.

4. DATA QUALITY NOTES
------------------------
- Co-directed films (Endgame/Infinity War; Frozen/Frozen II) list two
  Director credits in inconsistent order across films. Taking only the
  first would misattribute the film and split one team into two
  different "directors" depending on API order. Fixed by joining all
  Director credits with '|', sorted, so the same pair always
  canonicalizes the same way.
- spoken_languages falls back to English name where TMDb has no
  native-script name (e.g. Xhosa in the three Marvel films above).
- No duplicate rows, no missing id/title; all 18 fetched movies survived
  cleaning intact.

5. LIMITATIONS
-----------------
- 18 hand-picked blockbusters, not a representative sample -- aggregates
  describe this set, not the industry.
- Standalone group (n=2) and several genre ROI groups (1-3 movies each)
  are too small to generalize from.
- popularity/vote_count are live TMDb metrics and will drift on a
  fresh pull.

6. CONCLUSION
-----------------
Within this dataset, the Marvel/Avengers franchise wins by nearly every
measure: most franchise revenue, the top-rated directing team (Russo
brothers), and the top votes/popularity. James Cameron's Avatar and
Titanic post the single highest director-level revenue, driven by ROI
rather than budget size. Franchise films command bigger budgets and more
popularity here, but standalone films' ROI edge -- resting on only 2
data points -- should not be generalized beyond this sample.
