TMDb MOVIE DATA ANALYSIS -- FINAL REPORT
==========================================


1. OVERVIEW
------------

This project extracts, cleans, and analyzes data for 19 movies specified
in the project brief, using the TMDb API. It answers a set of KPI
questions (best/worst performers, franchise vs. standalone success, most
successful directors) and produces supporting visualizations.

Of the 19 movie IDs requested, 18 resolved to real TMDb records; ID 0 is
not a valid movie ID and was skipped automatically during fetching. All
figures in this report are therefore based on N = 18 movies.


2. METHODOLOGY
----------------

Step 1 -- Fetch: Each movie's core record was pulled from the TMDb
/movie/{id} endpoint and cached locally (data/raw/movies.json) so the
pipeline never re-fetches unnecessarily.

Step 2 -- Clean & Preprocess: Irrelevant/undocumented columns were
dropped (adult, imdb_id, original_title, video, homepage, softcore).
JSON-nested fields (genres, production_companies, production_countries,
spoken_languages, belongs_to_collection) were flattened into readable
'|'-joined strings. Data types were coerced (budget/id/popularity to
numeric, release_date to datetime). Budget/revenue/runtime values of
exactly 0 were treated as missing, not real zeros, and converted to
NaN. vote_average was blanked wherever vote_count was 0, since a rating
with no votes behind it isn't meaningful. Cast, cast size, director, and
crew size were enriched from the separate /credits endpoint.

Step 3 -- KPI Analysis: budget and revenue were converted to millions of
USD; profit (revenue - budget) and ROI (revenue / budget) were derived.
Rankings were computed with a single reusable ranking function so every
KPI (revenue, budget, profit, ROI, votes, rating, popularity) is
computed the same way. ROI rankings and the "rated" rankings apply the
brief's filters (budget >= 10M for ROI; vote_count >= 10 for rating) --
these are real defects to watch for, since a naive top-N without the
filter can pick a different answer than a filtered one on the same
data.

Step 4 -- Visualization: five charts (Revenue vs. Budget, ROI by Genre,
Popularity vs. Rating, Yearly Revenue Trend, Franchise vs. Standalone)
were rendered with Matplotlib and saved to reports/figures/.

The full pipeline is reproducible end to end via:
    python scripts/run_pipeline.py
and is covered by an automated test suite (43 tests, see tests/) that
exercises the cleaning, KPI, and fetch logic without hitting the real
API.


3. KEY INSIGHTS
------------------

Best / Worst Performing Movies
- Highest revenue: Avatar ($2,923.7M), Avengers: Endgame ($2,799.4M),
  Titanic ($2,264.2M).
- Highest budget: Avengers: Endgame ($356M), Avengers: Infinity War and
  Star Wars: The Last Jedi (tied at $300M).
- Highest profit: Avatar ($2,686.7M), Avengers: Endgame ($2,443.4M),
  Titanic ($2,064.2M) -- the same three movies lead both revenue and
  profit, since their budgets are proportionally smaller than their
  earnings.
- Lowest profit: Star Wars: The Last Jedi ($1,034.4M), Incredibles 2
  ($1,043.2M), Beauty and the Beast ($1,106.1M) -- still hugely
  profitable in absolute terms; "lowest" here only means lowest within
  this specific set of blockbusters, not unprofitable.
- Highest ROI (budget >= $10M): Avatar (12.3x), Titanic (11.3x),
  Jurassic World (11.1x).
- Lowest ROI (budget >= $10M): Star Wars: The Last Jedi (4.4x),
  Avengers: Age of Ultron (6.0x), Incredibles 2 (6.2x).
- Most voted: The Avengers (39,167 votes), Avatar (34,419), Avengers:
  Infinity War (32,531).
- Highest rated (>= 10 votes): Avengers: Infinity War (8.24), Avengers:
  Endgame (8.24), Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows: Part 2 (8.08).
- Lowest rated (>= 10 votes): Jurassic World: Fallen Kingdom (6.53),
  Jurassic World (6.70), Star Wars: The Last Jedi (6.74).
- Most popular (TMDb's live popularity metric): Avengers: Infinity War
  (92.0), The Avengers (77.9), Avengers: Endgame (65.2).
- Rating and popularity correlate moderately (r = 0.785) -- popular
  movies in this set do tend to be better-rated, though the two are far
  from identical.

Advanced Search Queries
- "Best-rated Sci-Fi Action movies starring Bruce Willis" and "movies
  starring Uma Thurman directed by Quentin Tarantino" both returned zero
  matches. This is expected, not a bug: the 18 movies in this dataset
  are the specific blockbuster franchise entries named in the project
  brief (Avengers, Avatar, Star Wars, Titanic, Jurassic World, etc.);
  titles like Die Hard or Pulp Fiction were never fetched. The filtering
  logic itself is written to work correctly against any larger dataset.

Franchise vs. Standalone Performance
- 16 of 18 movies belong to a franchise (belongs_to_collection is set);
  only 2 are standalone.
- Mean revenue is close either way (Standalone $1,765.1M vs. Franchise
  $1,682.8M), but median ROI favors standalone films (9.6x vs. 7.8x),
  and franchise films run a noticeably higher mean budget ($218M vs.
  $180M) and mean popularity (36.8 vs. 27.5). Caveat: this split is only
  2 standalone movies vs. 16 franchise movies, so the standalone side of
  this comparison is not statistically meaningful -- it describes these
  two specific movies, not "standalone films" in general.

Most Successful Movie Franchises (by total revenue)
1. The Avengers Collection -- 4 movies, $7,776.1M total revenue, 7.955
   average rating.
2. Star Wars Collection -- 2 movies, $3,402.6M, 6.995 average rating.
3. Jurassic Park Collection -- 2 movies, $2,982.0M, 6.617 average
   rating.
4. Avatar Collection -- 1 movie, $2,923.7M, 7.609 average rating.
5. Frozen Collection -- 2 movies, $2,727.9M, 7.238 average rating.

Most Successful Directors (by total revenue)
1. James Cameron -- 2 movies (Avatar, Titanic), $5,187.9M total revenue,
   7.755 average rating.
2. Joss Whedon -- 2 movies, $2,924.2M, 7.671 average rating.
3. Anthony Russo -- 1 movie (Avengers: Endgame), $2,799.4M, 8.239
   average rating -- the highest rating among these top 5 directors by
   revenue. Across all 15 directors in the dataset, Joe Russo (Avengers:
   Infinity War, 8.240) edges it out by a hair, and David Yates (Harry
   Potter and the Deathly Hallows: Part 2, 8.080) is close behind.
4. Jennifer Lee -- 2 movies, $2,727.9M, 7.238 average rating.
5. J.J. Abrams -- 1 movie, $2,068.2M, 7.245 average rating.


4. DATA QUALITY NOTES
------------------------

- spoken_languages: TMDb has no native-script name for some languages
  (Xhosa, spoken in Avengers: Endgame, Avengers: Infinity War, and Black
  Panther) -- only an English name. The cleaning step falls back to the
  English name for these instead of silently dropping the language or
  leaving a stray separator in the joined string.
- belongs_to_collection, production_companies/countries, and
  spoken_languages were all inspected with value_counts() after
  extraction specifically to catch anomalies like this before they
  reached the KPI stage.
- No duplicate rows, no missing id/title, and no movie had fewer than
  10 non-null columns -- the entire fetched set of 18 survived cleaning
  intact.


5. LIMITATIONS
-----------------

- Sample size: 18 movies is small, and all of them are high-profile
  blockbusters specified in the project brief -- not a random or
  representative sample of the movie industry. Aggregates (means,
  medians, correlations) describe this specific set, not movies in
  general.
- Franchise vs. standalone: the standalone group has only 2 movies;
  treat that comparison as illustrative, not statistically robust.
- ROI by genre (see reports/figures/roi_by_genre.png): most genres have
  only 1-3 data points, so those boxplots are similarly illustrative.
- Yearly revenue trend reflects the release years of these 18
  hand-picked movies, not actual industry-wide box office trends for
  those years.
- popularity and vote_count are live TMDb metrics that change over
  time; the exact values in this report reflect the dataset as fetched
  and will drift on a fresh pull.


6. CONCLUSIONS
-----------------

Within this dataset, the Marvel/Avengers franchise is the strongest
performer by nearly every measure: it has the most total revenue by
franchise, its directors (the Russo brothers, on Endgame and Infinity
War) hold the two highest average ratings of any director in the
dataset, and its movies dominate the votes and popularity rankings.
Avatar and Titanic (both James Cameron) combine for the single highest
director-level revenue total, driven almost entirely by exceptional ROI
rather than budget size -- both rank in the top 3 for ROI despite
mid-range budgets. Franchise films in general command bigger budgets
and more popularity than standalone films in this set, but standalone
films showed a better median ROI, though that finding rests on only 2
data points and should not be generalized.
